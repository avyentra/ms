AI Website Builder vs Hiring a Designer: How to Choose

AI website builder or a real designer? When each makes sense, what it really costs, and a simple 5-question way to decide.

Mangat Singh
18 Nov 20266 min read

You need a website, and every week you see another ad saying AI can build one in a few minutes. So it's fair to ask: why pay a designer at all?

I've designed 100+ websites and 60+ mobile apps over the past 8+ years, and I get this question from founders a lot now. My honest answer is that sometimes the AI builder is the right call. Other times it quietly costs you leads for a year before you notice. This post is about telling those two situations apart.

What AI website builders actually do well

Let's be fair to the tools first. An AI builder asks you a few questions, picks a template, writes some starter text and puts a site online. That's useful.

According to a guide on Website Builder Expert, AI builders can produce a site in under 10 minutes, for free or for a monthly plan (Wix, for example, ranges from $0 to $159 a month) (source). Note that the guide is sponsored by Wix, so read it with that in mind.

The tools are also getting stronger on the design side. In October 2026, Figma announced Figma Specs, a place to define a design language that teams and AI agents can follow, and said Figma Make is being connected to the Payload CMS for generating dynamic websites (TechCrunch). I use these tools myself. They speed up the boring parts.

So an AI builder makes sense when:

  • You're testing an idea and need a page live this week.
  • You have a single service, a simple offer and no complex flows.
  • Your budget is close to zero and you're fine writing the copy yourself.
  • The site is temporary, like an event page or a waitlist.

Where AI builders fall short

The same sources are clear about the limits. The Website Builder Expert guide lists generic designs, limited customization, ongoing payments and hidden fees from add-ons as the main downsides (source). A June 2026 piece in PC Tech Magazine adds that with DIY builders, the owner still has to write the copy and choose the photos, and that sites often stall half-finished or break on phones (PC Tech Magazine).

From my side, these are the problems I see most when a client comes to me after trying a builder:

1. The site looks like everyone else's

Templates are trained on what already exists. If your competitor used the same tool, your hero section may look almost identical. For a local dental clinic that may be fine. For a startup trying to look credible to investors, it isn't.

2. Nobody thought about the user's path

A builder can place sections. It doesn't ask: what does a first-time visitor need to see before they trust you? Where do they hesitate? Which button should be the main one? That thinking is what UX design actually is.

3. Mobile is "responsive" but not designed

Most builders squeeze the desktop layout onto a phone. Text wraps badly, buttons sit too close together, and the call to action ends up three screens down.

4. Anything custom gets painful

Booking flows, dashboards, member areas, product filters, multi-step forms. B12, which sells its own AI builder, still says builders "can't fully replace" projects with complex logic or unusual integrations (B12).

A simple way to decide

Ask yourself these five questions:

  1. Is the website how you get customers? If most leads come from Google, ads or referrals that check your site first, the design affects revenue. Get a designer.
  2. Do you have more than one type of user? Students and admins, buyers and sellers, patients and doctors. Multiple roles mean real UX work.
  3. Will you need anything beyond pages and a contact form? Logins, dashboards, e-commerce with filters, bookings. Builders get expensive or stuck here.
  4. Do you already know your message? If you can't explain your offer in one line yet, start with a cheap builder page and learn first.
  5. How long will this site live? A two-month campaign page and a five-year company site need different investments.

If you answered "yes" to the first three, a builder will probably hold you back. If you answered "no" to most of them, start with a builder and save your money.

There's also a middle path

You don't have to choose all or nothing. Many of my clients start with:

  • A designed landing page first, built around one goal, then the full site later.
  • A design-only project in Figma, which their own developer or builder then implements.
  • A redesign of key pages (home, pricing, contact) instead of the whole site.

What hiring a designer really costs

Cost depends heavily on where the designer is based. The US-focused articles above give ranges such as $1,000 to $3,000 for a freelancer-built site and $1,500 to $8,000 for an agency, plus monthly fees (PC Tech Magazine). B12 says US freelance developers commonly charge $50 to $150 an hour (B12).

Working with a designer in India changes that maths. I work at $15 USD per hour, and I quote landing pages, websites and apps per project after a short call, because scope matters more than page count.

Also compare over time, not just day one. A "free" builder plus premium plan, add-ons and apps is a monthly cost forever. A designed site is mostly an upfront cost.

How this played out on real projects

Two projects from my own work show where a designer adds something a template can't.

p448 Store (automotive e-commerce). An online store for car products needs a premium look and fast loading, because buyers compare a lot before purchasing. Getting both right together is design and build work, not template picking.

RBU Portal (education web app for Rayat Bahra University). A university portal has several types of users and many tasks inside one product. That's the kind of project where an AI builder simply has no template to start from. The work was in planning screens and flows so different users could find what they need. Rohit Sharma, the founder, shared a testimonial about the project on my website.

You can see both on ms.primeznews.com.

FAQ

Can I start with an AI builder and hire a designer later?

Yes, and for early-stage ideas I often recommend it. Just keep your content (text, photos, logo) in your own files so moving later is easy.

Will a designer use AI tools too?

Most do now, including me. The difference is that AI speeds up production, while the designer decides what should exist, in what order, and why.

How long does a custom designed website take?

It depends on scope. The Website Builder Expert guide puts freelancer sites at four to 12 weeks (source). A focused landing page is usually much faster. I give a timeline after understanding your project.
